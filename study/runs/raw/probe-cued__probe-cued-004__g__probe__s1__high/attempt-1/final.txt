<thoughtml version="1.0">
  <task>
    <title>Coastal Evacuation Decision Under Storm Uncertainty</title>
    <date>2026-08-21</date>
    <decision_type>Emergency management</decision_type>
  </task>

  <situation>
    <summary>A coastal emergency team must decide whether to order evacuation before storm landfall. The storm could intensify, road flooding may make later departure unsafe or impossible, and evacuation itself carries significant risk for medically fragile residents. A newer forecast reduces expected storm surge, but uncertainty remains material.</summary>
  </situation>

  <evidence>
    <item id="e1" supports="evacuation" weight="high">The storm may intensify before landfall, which could sharply worsen surge, wind, and life-safety risk on short notice.</item>
    <item id="e2" supports="early_action" weight="high">Roads could flood if departure is delayed, reducing evacuation capacity and increasing the chance of people becoming trapped.</item>
    <item id="e3" supports="caution" weight="high">Evacuation itself creates nontrivial harm for medically fragile residents, including transport stress, interrupted care, and sheltering complications.</item>
    <item id="e4" supports="partial_restraint" weight="medium">The newer forecast lowers expected surge, reducing the immediate case for a full-area mandatory evacuation.</item>
    <item id="e5" supports="preparedness" weight="medium">Forecast uncertainty remains high enough that relying on the lower-surge scenario alone would be unsafe.</item>
  </evidence>

  <counterevidence>
    <item id="c1" challenges="full_evacuation" weight="high">The reduced surge forecast weakens the justification for imposing the largest-scale evacuation now.</item>
    <item id="c2" challenges="delay" weight="high">Waiting for more certainty could backfire if intensification occurs after roads begin flooding.</item>
    <item id="c3" challenges="mandatory_order" weight="medium">A mandatory evacuation may expose medically fragile residents to avoidable danger if the storm underperforms.</item>
  </counterevidence>

  <options>
    <option id="o1" status="not_selected">
      <name>Immediate full mandatory evacuation</name>
      <pros>
        <point>Maximizes lead time before potential road flooding.</point>
        <point>Best protects against a late forecast worsening.</point>
      </pros>
      <cons>
        <point>May overreact to the updated lower-surge forecast.</point>
        <point>Imposes high evacuation risk on medically fragile residents.</point>
      </cons>
    </option>

    <option id="o2" status="selected">
      <name>Targeted evacuation now, full order on triggers</name>
      <pros>
        <point>Protects the most exposed zones and medically fragile residents who need extra time and support.</point>
        <point>Preserves flexibility while uncertainty resolves.</point>
        <point>Reduces the chance that later road flooding blocks movement.</point>
      </pros>
      <cons>
        <point>Requires clear trigger thresholds and disciplined execution.</point>
        <point>Some residents may misread a targeted order as a signal that broader risk is low.</point>
      </cons>
    </option>

    <option id="o3" status="not_selected">
      <name>Wait for another forecast update before any evacuation order</name>
      <pros>
        <point>Avoids unnecessary displacement if the weaker scenario holds.</point>
      </pros>
      <cons>
        <point>Risks losing the evacuation window if intensification or road flooding begins sooner than expected.</point>
        <point>Provides too little lead time for medically fragile populations.</point>
      </cons>
    </option>
  </options>

  <decision status="provisional">
    <current_decision>Do not issue a full mandatory evacuation at this time. Issue a targeted evacuation for the most surge-prone and access-constrained areas, prioritize assisted movement for medically fragile residents, and prepare to escalate rapidly to a broader mandatory evacuation if forecast or flooding triggers are met.</current_decision>
    <rationale>The lower-surge forecast reduces the case for immediate full-area evacuation, but uncertainty and the risk of losing safe road access make pure delay unacceptable. A targeted evacuation best balances false-alarm costs against the consequences of acting too late.</rationale>
  </decision>

  <triggers>
    <trigger>If forecast confidence shifts toward higher surge or stronger pre-landfall intensification, escalate to broader mandatory evacuation.</trigger>
    <trigger>If roadway flooding begins or evacuation route reliability deteriorates, escalate immediately.</trigger>
    <trigger>If transport and shelter support for medically fragile residents cannot be completed safely under the current timeline, expand protective action sooner.</trigger>
  </triggers>

  <confidence>
    <level>moderate</level>
    <note>The decision is intentionally reversible upward because the main residual risk is late deterioration combined with shrinking evacuation capacity.</note>
  </confidence>
